About me

About my research

Developmental Algorithms for Natural Behaviors

Studying population dynamics in development, linking neural computation to behavior beyond the level of single cells.

My work aims to (i) identify the developmental algorithms by which neural computations emerge from developing circuits, (ii) establish how they are calibrated by the developing body and brain states, and (iii) determine how they become anchored to the world and the ecological niche in which animals develop.

From the earliest days of postnatal life, developing animals already produce a rich repertoire of behaviors. We still lack an account of the population-level computations that developing circuits perform, and of how they support this early behavioral repertoire.

My research will address this gap by combining high-density electrophysiology in developing animals with 3D reconstruction of behavior and physiological state, analysis of population geometry, and cell-type-specific genetic manipulations. We will uncover the developmental algorithms for sensory, motor and cognitive functions that give rise to natural behaviors.

  1. Formation

    How does population geometry emerge from developing circuitry?

  2. Calibration

    How does it acquire its dynamics from a developing body?

  3. Anchoring

    How does it become referenced to the animal’s world and ecological niche?
